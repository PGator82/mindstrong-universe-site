How Spread Out Is Your Data?

Two classes both have a mean test score of 75. But in Class A, everyone scored between 70 and 80. In Class B, scores ranged from 40 to 100. The mean looks the same — but the spread is completely different. Measures of spread tell you how consistent or variable a data set really is.

The simplest measure is the range — the gap between the highest and lowest values. More advanced measures like the interquartile range (IQR) focus on the middle 50% of data, ignoring extreme outliers that can distort the picture.

The Hack

📏
Range
Subtract the minimum value from the maximum value. Quick to calculate but sensitive to extreme outliers.
📦
IQR
Interquartile Range = Q3 − Q1. Measures the spread of the middle 50% of data, ignoring extreme values.
🚨
Outliers
Values that are unusually far from the rest. They inflate the range and can skew the mean significantly.
⚡ The Rule
Range = Max − Min · IQR = Q3 − Q1 · Outlier check: value is suspect if it's more than 1.5 × IQR beyond Q1 or Q3

Practice

Q1: What is the range of [4, 7, 10, 15, 22]?
Q2: In a data set, Q1 = 10 and Q3 = 30. What is the IQR?
Q3: Data set: [5, 6, 7, 8, 100]. Which value is most likely an outlier?

Exit Check

What is the range of [3, 5, 7, 9, 12]?

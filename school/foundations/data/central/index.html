The Three Anchors of Data

Imagine your class just got test scores back: 70, 85, 90, 90, 95. How do you summarize those five numbers with just one? That single-number summary is called a measure of central tendency — it tells you where the "center" of your data lives.

There are three classic measures: the mean (the arithmetic average), the median (the middle value when sorted), and the mode (the value that appears most often). Each tells a slightly different story, and knowing which to use is a superpower in data analysis.

The Hack

➕
Mean
Add all values, then divide by how many there are. Best for evenly spread data with no extreme outliers.
🎯
Median
Sort the list, find the middle value. If two middle values exist, average them. Resistant to outliers.
🔁
Mode
The value that appears most frequently. A data set can have one mode, multiple modes, or no mode at all.
⚡ The Rule
Mean = (sum of all values) ÷ (count of values) · Median = middle value after sorting · Mode = most frequent value

Practice

Q1: What is the mean of [4, 6, 8, 10, 12]?
Q2: What is the median of [3, 7, 9, 15, 21]?
Q3: What is the mode of [5, 3, 5, 8, 5, 3, 1]?
